Word Emphasis

Discover how stressing different words changes the meaning of a sentence

In English, you can change the entire meaning of a sentence just by emphasizing (stressing) a different word. This is called contrastive stress. Below you'll see how the same sentence can have seven different meanings depending on which word is stressed. Read the examples, then try the quiz!

🔎 Example: "I didn't say he stole the money."

Click any sentence to hear it (spoken neutrally). Focus on how the bold word would change the meaning if emphasized.

I didn't say he stole the money.
Meaning: Someone else said it, not me.
I didn't say he stole the money.
Meaning: I definitely did not say it.
I didn't say he stole the money.
Meaning: I might have implied it, but I didn't say it aloud.
I didn't say he stole the money.
Meaning: Someone else stole it, not him.
I didn't say he stole the money.
Meaning: He took the money, but maybe he borrowed it.
I didn't say he stole the money.
Meaning: He stole something else, not the money.

🗣️ More Emphasis in Everyday Speech

Stressing a word can also express surprise, correct someone, or show contrast.

She is coming to the party?
Surprise: "She? I thought her sister was coming."
I want the red dress, not the blue one.
Contrast: distinguishing between two options.
I do like it!
Emphatic agreement: correcting someone who thinks you don't.
